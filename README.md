## Abstract

> The distinction between authentic random numbers and those generated by humans is a critical challenge with significant implications in fields such as digital forensics, financial auditing, and data security. Human-generated numbers, even when intended to be random, are inherently biased by cognitive shortcuts and psychological patterns, such as the avoidance of repeating digits or a preference for certain numbers. This project presents a novel algorithm, developed in Python, that leverages machine learning to detect these subtle human signatures. By analyzing a dataset of numerical sequences, our model is trained to classify numbers as either "human-made" or "authentically random." The system extracts key statistical and psychological features from the input numbers and uses a trained classifier to make its prediction. The primary application of this tool is in forensic accounting, where it can be used to verify the authenticity of financial data, such as transaction amounts or inventory counts, by flagging potentially fabricated figures. This work demonstrates the potential of AI in augmenting traditional forensic methods, providing a quantifiable and automated means to enhance the integrity and reliability of numerical data across various domains.

## 1. Problem Formulation & Motivation for AI

### 1.1 The Forensic Accounting Context
In forensic accounting and fraud investigation, bad actors often fabricate invoice numbers, expense vouchers, transaction amounts, or inventory counts out of thin air. While fraudsters attempt to make these numbers appear completely haphazard and "random," human psychology prevents them from producing true stochastic distributions.

Humans suffer from well-documented cognitive biases when generating numbers:
- **Repetition Avoidance (Alternation Bias):** Humans disproportionately avoid repeating identical adjacent digits (e.g., `55`, `777`) under the mistaken belief (Gambler's Fallacy) that true randomness does not cluster.
- **Positional & Favorite Digit Biases:** Humans favor certain digits (e.g., `7`, `3`) while neglecting others (e.g., `0`, `5`), and frequently mirror calendar years or familiar patterns (e.g., `19xx`, `20xx`).
- **Benford's Law Deviations:** In financial records, naturally occurring numbers typically conform to Benford's Law (logarithmic distribution of leading digits). Fabricated numbers frequently deviate sharply from expected Benford distributions.

### 1.2 Why Plain Scripts & Lookup Tables Fail
A deterministic script, regex, or static lookup table cannot capture the subtle, high-dimensional interplay of these biases across varying sequence lengths and batch sizes:
- A single number alone (e.g., `4821`) might appear plausible under both human and PRNG conditions.
- Human biases manifest as **probabilistic tendencies**, not hard deterministic rules.
- Fabricators can easily circumvent a static rule (such as `no repeating digits`), but cannot evade the multi-order probabilistic signatures captured by machine learning and information theory.

## 3. Core Technique Implemented from Scratch

In adherence to the project guidelines, **all core learning and inference algorithms are implemented strictly from scratch using standard Python (`math`, `random`, `collections.Counter`) without relying on black-box ML frameworks (such as `scikit-learn` or `PyTorch`).**

### 3.1 Stage 1: Positional & Adjacent-Digram Naive Bayes
For any candidate sequence $s = c_0 c_1 c_2 c_3$:
- **Positional Likelihood:** Evaluates the probability of observing digit $c_i$ at index $i$, smoothed via Laplace add-1 smoothing:
  $$\hat{P}_{\text{human}}(c_i \mid \text{pos}) = \frac{\text{Count}(c_i, \text{pos}) + \alpha}{N + \alpha \cdot K}$$
- **Digram Transition Likelihood:** Evaluates the transition between adjacent digits $c_i c_{i+1}$:
  $$\hat{P}_{\text{human}}(c_i c_{i+1}) = \frac{\text{Count}(c_i c_{i+1}) + 0.5}{N_{\text{digram}} + 0.5 \cdot 100}$$
- **Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR):** Calculates the log-odds ratio comparing human likelihood against a uniform random generator baseline.

### 3.2 Stage 2: Cross-Entropy & Multiplicity Deviations
Extracts three information-theoretic feature deviations:
1. `dev_dig`: Divergence between item digit frequency and human empirical digit distribution compared to uniform entropy.
2. `dev_mult`: Divergence between max digit multiplicity $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ relative to theoretical PRNG frequencies:
   $$\text{RAND\_MULT} = \left\{1: \frac{4536}{9000},\; 2: \frac{4131}{9000},\; 3: \frac{324}{9000},\; 4: \frac{9}{9000}\right\}$$
3. `dev_benf`: Cross-entropy deviation of the first digit against Benford's distribution:
   $$P_{\text{Benford}}(d) = \log_{10}\left(1 + \frac{1}{d}\right), \quad d \in \{1, \dots, 9\}$$

### 3.3 Bayesian Joint Batch Likelihood Compounding
For an entire batch of $k$ items $B = [x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k]$, individual item likelihoods are aggregated under the conditional independence assumption:

$$P(B \mid \text{Fake}) = \prod_{i=1}^k \prod_{j=1}^4 \mathcal{N}(f_j(x_i);\, \mu_{j, \text{fake}},\, \sigma_{j, \text{fake}})$$

$$P(B \mid \text{Real}) = \prod_{i=1}^k \prod_{j=1}^4 \mathcal{N}(f_j(x_i);\, \mu_{j, \text{real}},\, \sigma_{j, \text{real}})$$

Applying Bayes' Theorem with prior $P(\text{Fake}) = 0.5$:

$$P(\text{Fake} \mid B) = \frac{P(\text{Fake}) \cdot P(B \mid \text{Fake})}{P(\text{Fake}) \cdot P(B \mid \text{Fake}) + P(\text{Real}) \cdot P(B \mid \text{Real})}$$

Calculations are computed entirely in log-space with max-subtraction normalization to eliminate floating-point underflow.

## 7. Experimental Results & Analysis

| Batch Size ($k$) | Validation Scheme | Classification Accuracy | Avg $P(\text{Fake})$ on Human | Avg $P(\text{Fake})$ on PRNG |
|:----------------:|:-----------------:|:-----------------------:|:-----------------------------:|:----------------------------:|
| **$k = 1$** (Baseline) | 5-Fold CV (20 seeds) | **58.0%** | 0.543 | 0.435 |
| **$k = 5$** | 4-Fold CV (20 seeds) | **73.4%** | 0.666 | 0.287 |
| **$k = 10$** | 4-Fold CV (20 seeds) | **75.6%** | 0.717 | 0.209 |

### Key Insight: The Compound Evidence Principle
- When assessing a **single number** ($k=1$), accuracy is modest (58.0%) because individual 4-digit human sequences often overlap with valid random draws.
- When aggregated across an accounting batch ($k=10$), the subtle human biases (e.g., absence of repeated digits, avoidance of round numbers, and year prefixes) **compound exponentially** across independent log-likelihoods.
- In the live demo batch of 10 items, the suspected human batch yields a **92.73% posterior probability of fraud**, whereas the true PRNG batch registers **0.01%**, providing forensic auditors with a high-confidence indicator.

## 8. Failure Analysis & System Limitations

1. **Short Sequences vs. Long Sequences:**
   - On isolated single numbers ($k=1$), high variance and limited sample length (4 digits) constrain accuracy. True random numbers that happen to have no repeating digits can occasionally trigger false positives.
2. **Adversarially Sophisticated Humans:**
   - If an employee intentionally uses a local random number generator or copy-pastes dice rolls, the system will classify the numbers as authentically random. The model detects *human cognitive shortcuts*, not the unauthorized entry itself.
3. **Domain-Specific Accounting Formats:**
   - In financial data, specific transaction types naturally gravitate toward fixed thresholds (e.g., \$99.99 pricing or corporate card limits of \$1,000). The model must be fine-tuned or conditioned on domain-specific distributions to avoid misinterpreting legitimate operational patterns as human fraud.

## 9. Societal Considerations & Ethical Impact

- **Beneficiaries:**
  - Forensic auditors, tax authorities, and compliance teams can rapidly audit thousands of records, uncovering expense skimming, payroll fabrication, and inventory padding.
  - Small businesses and non-profits benefit from automated oversight tools that protect against internal embezzlement without requiring expensive proprietary auditing suites.
- **Risks & Ethical Safeguards:**
  - **Automation Bias:** Investigators must not treat model predictions as definitive proof of criminal intent. The system is designed as a *screening and triage tool* to flag anomalies for human audit, not as an autonomous punitive decision-maker.
  - **Presumption of Innocence:** High fake probabilities indicate statistical anomalies consistent with human generation, which may occasionally occur due to legitimate business conventions or edge-case randomness.
